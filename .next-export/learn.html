how 2 trade — the six rules

  1. 1. Pick a company card on the board. The ink line is its price, drawn live.
  2. 2. Buying fills you at the ask (slightly higher). Selling fills at the bid (slightly lower). That gap is the spread — the cost of trading right now.
  3. 3. Every order pays a $0.99 paperwork fee. Tiny trades get eaten by fees — size matters.
  4. 4. Big orders push prices. Try buying max shares of Cringold and watch your own footprint.
  5. 5. Sticky notes are news — gossip shocks prices. The market mood sways all eight stocks at once.
  6. 6. Your report card is the pencil line in the Portfolio: beat the PAPER 500 and you're officially good at paper.
  7. 7. Betting against: SHORT borrows shares and sells them — you profit if the price falls. Buy them back (COVER) to win or admit defeat. Losses on shorts have no floor, so the cap is 25% of your assets.

the glossary wall

bid / askbid = what buyers offer you; ask = what sellers demand. You sell at the bid, buy at the ask.
spreadthe gap between bid and ask. It's the toll booth of trading — you pay it on every round trip.
paperwork fee$0.99 per order, like a real commission. Tiny trades pay proportionally more.
market impactyour own big orders shove the price against you. Whales make waves.
volatilityhow violently a price swings: calm → steady → jumpy → wild. Wild pays double — both ways.
betahow strongly a stock follows the market's mood. Beta 1.4 = 40% extra swing with the tide.
moodthe market's weather. In panicky regimes almost everything falls together — that's correlation.
circuit breakera >8% single-tick move pauses that stock for a few ticks, so nobody faceplants mid-fall.
PAPER 500all 8 stocks, equally weighted — the 'just hold everything' benchmark you're graded against.
realized vs unrealizedrealized = the ink is dry (you sold). unrealized = pencil (you could still lose it).
diversificationsplit cash across 3–4 companies so one faceplant doesn't sink the whole fleet.
margin loanborrowing to buy more paper. Gains and losses both get bigger, and interest accrues daily.
short sellingborrow shares, sell them now, hope to buy them back cheaper. Profit = entry − cover price. The moose frowns when you abuse it.
margin callif a short grows past half your assets, the bank force-buys it back (dusting your holdings to pay if needed). Nobody warns you twice.
reward checkssome mornings Moorthy's mails a paper dividend to your desk. Sign it with your mouse-pencil to bank it.
IPOinitial public offering — sell 40 shares of your factory to the crowd at a fair price, keep 60 founder shares. Run the mill well and the stock follows.

do ✓

  • spread across a few names — diversification
  • read the order preview before you commit
  • take some profits sometimes; realized beats imagined
  • check the volatility word before sizing up
  • grade yourself against the PAPER 500, not vibes
  • short euphoric moods if you like — bubbles pop eventually

don't ✗

  • don't go max-size on a ‘wild’ stock (hi, Cringold)
  • don't buy 3 shares — the $0.99 fee will eat you
  • don't panic-sell every red tick; check the mood first
  • don't borrow until the basics feel boring
  • don't mistake a bull mood for being a genius
  • don't short a ‘wild’ stock big — profits cap at zero, losses don't
